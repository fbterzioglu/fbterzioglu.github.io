How I work

Profile

The pattern that repeats across my work is a method rather than a technology. I take an open question — can this metadata be standardized automatically, can a group of agents draft a defensible due-diligence report, which model should sit behind this product — and I build an evaluation harness to answer it before I commit to an architecture. Then I carry the answer into production.

That second half is the part I care about. Several of these answers now run on live Kubernetes, on schedules, with cost ceilings and failure handling. One of them — a document-generation engine I wrote for a small internal tool — was later adopted wholesale by the company's flagship pipeline. I would rather build a capability once and see it travel than ship a demo that impresses and then rots.

I work at both ends of the collaboration spectrum: sole author of six focused systems, and lead contributor on larger team platforms. The surface area is wide for a research role — Python backends, React and TypeScript front ends, Kubernetes deployment, model training and fine-tuning, formal benchmark design — because in applied research the gap between a result and a system is usually where the interesting problems live.

My domain is Turkish legal language. It is a genuinely hard setting: morphologically rich, under-resourced relative to English, and unforgiving about correctness, since a wrong citation is not a rounding error but a professional liability. Much of my work is about closing that gap — training Turkish retrieval and embedding models, grounding every generated claim to a source, and building the ingestion layer beneath it all.

Publications

Four papers · 2025 — 2026

Co-authored research with the NewMind AI research group, concentrated on making language models work in Turkish — retrieval, hallucination detection, and efficient adaptation. Full listing on Google Scholar.

  1. arXiv:2604.19321 · April 2026

    RDP LoRA: Geometry-Driven Identification for Parameter-Efficient Adaptation in Large Language Models

    Yusuf Çelebi, Yağız Asker, Özay Ezerceli, Mahmoud ElHussieni, Selva Taş, Reyhan Bayraktar, Fatma Betül Terzioğlu

    Models the evolution of hidden states as a high-dimensional geometric trajectory and applies the Ramer–Douglas–Peucker algorithm — parameter-free and training-free — to identify which layers actually warrant adaptation. Using 13 geometrically selected layers of Qwen3-8B-Base reaches 81.67% on MMLU-Math, beating full 36-layer adaptation (79.32%), random 13-layer selection (75.56%), and the base model (74.25%).

  2. SSRN 6919461 · 2026

    Agentology: Ontology-Driven Operational Environments for Multi-Agent Systems

    Uğur Ortaç, E. Tosun, A. K. Özbek, Fatma Betül Terzioğlu, Reyhan Bayraktar

    Proposes ontology-driven operational environments as a structuring principle for multi-agent systems — the conceptual counterpart to the agent platforms described below.

  3. arXiv:2511.16528 · November 2025

    TurkColBERT: A Benchmark of Dense and Late-Interaction Models for Turkish Information Retrieval

    Özay Ezerceli, Mahmoud El Hussieni, Selva Taş, Reyhan Bayraktar, Fatma Betül Terzioğlu, Yusuf Çelebi, Yağız Asker

    A benchmark of ten models across five Turkish datasets spanning scientific, financial and argumentative domains, using a two-stage adaptation pipeline. The headline finding is a case for late interaction in lower-resource languages: a 1.0M-parameter late-interaction model matches a dense encoder 600× its size, with MUVERA+Rerank indexing delivering sub-millisecond query latency.

  4. arXiv:2509.17671 · September 2025

    Turk-LettuceDetect: Hallucination Detection Models for Turkish RAG Applications

    Selva Taş, Mahmut El Huseyni, Özay Ezerceli, Reyhan Bayraktar, Fatma Betül Terzioğlu

    Hallucination detection for Turkish retrieval-augmented generation: three encoder architectures fine-tuned on a machine-translated RAGTruth corpus of 17,790 instances, with the best model reaching an F1 of 0.7266 over contexts up to 8,192 tokens while staying computationally cheap enough to sit in a live pipeline.

Production news intelligence pipeline

An end-to-end system that ingests roughly 56 sources across RSS, sitemaps and browser scraping, filters and summarizes them through staged language-model calls, deduplicates, persists to a document store, and delivers finished briefings through messaging, email, and an automatically typeset journal rendered to DOCX, PDF and slides. A separate control plane handles administration and monitoring.

I designed and built the whole system — ingestion, the model stages, persistence, per-stage cost accounting, every delivery channel, the document generator, and the containerized deployment. It ships with a full test suite and runs unattended on a daily, weekly and monthly cadence.

Design decision

A hard monthly inference budget with an automatic circuit-breaker is built into the pipeline, not bolted on afterwards. An LLM system without a cost ceiling is a liability in production, and the ceiling has to be a first-class component to be trusted.

Multi-agent platform for legal due diligence

A hierarchical retrieval-augmented system: an orchestrator decomposes a legal question into sub-tasks; parallel subagents retrieve over hybrid dense and full-text search and draft cited subreports; a reviewer agent grounds every claim against its citation; an aggregator merges the results; a report writer assembles the deliverable with a provenance map and a visualization of the agent tree.

My contributions centred on the parts that make it trustworthy and deployable: the citation and source-highlighting system, multi-tenant isolation across the backend, interface, pipeline and ontology layers, a restructured prompt-management scheme, embedding-model swaps with GPU support, authored analysis skills for contract comparison and case-law lookup, and the CI/CD pipeline.

Result

I bridged the platform to an external legal-AI benchmark scored by an independent model judge — replacing self-assessment with a measure we did not control.

Python · hierarchical agent orchestration · hybrid retrieval (dense + full-text with rank fusion) · sentence-transformers · FAISS · A2A protocol · OCR · LaTeX · Docker

Automated metadata standardization

A system that ingests heterogeneous documents — spreadsheets, PDF indexes, scanned record catalogs — extracts their core descriptive fields, enriches them with a language model, and emits standardized Dublin Core records through a seven-stage pipeline: extraction, semantic clustering, field cleaning, model enrichment, and identifier filtering.

I designed and built every component: the vector-based semantic clusterer, a hybrid rule/model field cleaner, an identifier filter that keeps dates and reference codes from polluting the clustering signal, and a clean-architecture refactor with a typed React front end. The work ran from open research through production deployment and testing, and was then extended into a Neo4j knowledge graph and delivered to the business.

Python · FastAPI · pydantic · sentence-transformers · FAISS · networkx · pandas · React 19 · TypeScript · Vite · Neo4j · pytest

Long-context reasoning without retrieval

A deliberate counter-experiment to the retrieval orthodoxy running through the rest of my work: reasoning over up to 100 documents per session inside a 196K-token window with no embeddings and no vector database at all — sliding windows, map-reduce, context compaction, a hard cost ceiling, and a hallucination and citation guard.

Why build it

RAG is the reflex answer for document reasoning, and reflexes deserve testing. As context windows grow, the honest question is which problems still need retrieval — and that question is only answerable if someone builds the alternative and measures it.

Python · FastAPI · tiktoken · sliding-window map-reduce · context compaction · async LLM routing
